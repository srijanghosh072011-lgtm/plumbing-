How to Unclog a Drain: A Step-by-Step Guide for Calgary Homeowners

June 23, 2026 · 8 min read · By 24/7 Plumbing & Heating Ltd. · Reviewed by a licensed Calgary plumber

A slow-draining sink or a completely blocked shower is one of the most common plumbing calls we receive from Calgary homeowners — and most of the time, it's something you can fix yourself in under 20 minutes with tools already in your home. This guide walks you through every safe DIY method, from the gentlest to the most involved, and tells you exactly when the problem is too deep to DIY.

Calgary-specific note: Calgary's municipal water supply is notoriously hard — typically 200–250 mg/L of calcium carbonate, one of the highest concentrations in Canada. Over time this leaves mineral deposits (limescale) inside your pipes that narrow the opening and make every clog worse. We'll address this throughout the guide.

Why Do Drains Clog? (Common Calgary Causes)

Before reaching for a tool, it helps to know what you're dealing with. The most common causes by drain type:

Drain LocationMost Common Cause
Bathroom sinkHair + soap scum bonded together; limescale narrowing the stopper
Shower/bathHair; shampoo & conditioner residue; body wash scum
Kitchen sinkGrease and cooking fat; food particles; coffee grounds
Floor drainSediment, lint, mineral buildup from Calgary's hard water
ToiletExcessive toilet paper; wipes (never flushable, despite the label)
All drains slowMain sewer line blockage — always call a plumber

Tools You Need (and What to Skip)

You do not need to buy anything for most drain clogs. Here's what works — and what to avoid:

  • Rubber gloves — always wear them. Drain water carries bacteria.
  • Cup plunger — the classic rubber bell on a stick. Not a flange plunger (that's for toilets).
  • Drain snake (hand auger) — a coiled metal cable on a hand crank. Available at Canadian Tire for under $25.
  • Needle-nose pliers or a drain claw — for grabbing visible hair clogs near the top.
  • Adjustable pliers — only needed if you're cleaning the P-trap.
  • Baking soda + white vinegar — gentle and safe for regular maintenance.

What to avoid:

  • Boiling water directly on PVC pipes — thermal shock can crack fittings.
  • Bleach in drains — doesn't dissolve clogs, creates toxic fumes if mixed with ammonia-based cleaners.
  • Chemical drain cleaners (Drano, Liquid-Plumr) for recurring clogs — they damage pipes over time and are a temporary fix at best.

Method 1 — Bathroom Sink Clog

This is the most common call we get. The fix is almost always visible hair bonded to soap scum around the drain stopper.

  1. Remove the stopper. Most Calgary bathroom sinks have either a lift-and-turn stopper (twist counterclockwise and lift) or a pop-up stopper connected to a pivot rod under the sink (unscrew the horizontal pivot rod nut and the stopper lifts free).
  2. Clear the stopper. It will almost certainly be coated in hair and soap. Clean it thoroughly before reinstalling.
  3. Check the drain opening. Shine a flashlight in. Use needle-nose pliers or a drain claw to pull out any hair matted just inside the opening.
  4. Baking soda + vinegar flush. Pour ½ cup baking soda into the drain, then ½ cup white vinegar. Cover the drain opening with the stopper or a damp cloth for 15 minutes. Flush with hot tap water (not boiling).
  5. Plunge if needed. Fill the basin with 2–3 inches of water. Block the overflow drain (usually a small hole near the top of the basin) with a damp cloth — this creates a proper seal. Place the cup plunger over the drain, push down to expel air, then pump 10–15 times vigorously. Pull away sharply on the last stroke.

If water flows freely after step 3 or 5, you're done. If not, move to the snake (Method 4).

Method 2 — Shower or Bath Drain

Hair is the #1 culprit. Unlike bathroom sinks, shower drains rarely have a stopper you can remove easily, but you can still access the clog.

  1. Remove the drain cover. Most unscrew with a flat-head screwdriver. Some just pull up with a pry tool. Set it aside.
  2. Extract visible hair. Use a drain claw (a plastic zip-it tool from Canadian Tire costs about $3) or needle-nose pliers. It's unpleasant, but it works instantly for most shower clogs.
  3. Baking soda + vinegar treatment. Same as above — ½ cup each, wait 15 minutes, flush with hot water.
  4. Plunge. Unlike a sink, you don't need to block an overflow hole. Just plunge firmly 10–15 times with a cup plunger.
Prevention tip: A $5 silicone hair catcher over your shower drain is the single most effective drain maintenance investment you can make. Calgary households with long hair occupants should clean it weekly.

Method 3 — Kitchen Sink Clog

Kitchen drain clogs are almost always caused by grease and fat that cooled and solidified in the pipe. The baking soda + vinegar trick is less effective here because grease is not water-soluble. Use this approach instead:

  1. Never pour grease down the drain. If you already did — this clog is the result.
  2. Hot dish soap flush. Squirt a generous amount of dish soap into the drain, then pour the hottest water your tap can produce. Dish soap is a degreaser and it works better than baking soda on fat.
  3. Salt + hot water. Pour ½ cup table salt into the drain, then flush with a kettle of very hot water. The salt acts as a mild abrasive and helps break up the grease layer. Do not use boiling water if your sink has PVC drain pipes — test a small amount first.
  4. Plunge. If your kitchen sink has two basins, plug the second basin with a stopper or damp cloth before plunging — otherwise you'll just push water back and forth between basins instead of clearing the clog.
  5. Clean the P-trap. Kitchen clogs that don't respond to plunging are almost always in the P-trap (the curved U-shaped pipe directly under the sink). Place a bucket beneath it, unscrew the slip-joint nuts by hand (or with pliers if corroded), remove the trap, and clean it out. Most kitchen P-trap clogs are a dense mass of grease, food, and soap residue.

Method 4 — Drain Snake (for Stubborn Clogs)

When plunging fails, a hand auger (drain snake) reaches 15–25 feet into the pipe — well past the P-trap and into the main drain line where some clogs hide.

  1. Feed the coiled end of the snake into the drain opening (or directly into the drain pipe if you've removed the P-trap).
  2. Turn the handle clockwise as you push forward. You'll feel resistance when you hit the clog.
  3. Continue turning and pushing gently until you feel the snake break through or hook the clog. Do not force it — too much pressure can damage plastic pipe fittings.
  4. Rotate the handle counterclockwise a few times, then pull back slowly. The clog material will wrap around the coil.
  5. Pull the snake out, clean off the debris, and repeat if needed.
  6. Run hot water for 1–2 minutes to flush the pipe clear.

Method 5 — Toilet Clogs

Always use a flange plunger for a toilet (the one with the rubber flap that extends from the bell) — a cup plunger doesn't seal properly on a toilet bowl.

  1. Turn off the water supply valve (behind the toilet, near the floor) if the bowl is close to overflowing.
  2. Place the flange plunger into the bowl so the rubber flap seals inside the drain opening at the bottom.
  3. Push down gently first to remove air from the bell, then pump firmly 10–15 times in a steady rhythm.
  4. For stubborn toilet clogs, a toilet auger (also called a closet auger) is the right tool — it has a protective rubber sleeve to avoid scratching the porcelain. Never use a standard drain snake in a toilet.
What to never flush in Calgary: Wet wipes (even "flushable" ones don't break down in Calgary's sewer system), paper towels, cotton pads, dental floss, Q-tips, or any hygiene product other than toilet paper.

Calgary Hard Water and Your Drains

Calgary's Bow and Elbow River source water is naturally hard. As it flows through your home, calcium and magnesium minerals precipitate out and form limescale deposits on every surface they contact — including the inside of your drain pipes.

Over 5–10 years in an older home, limescale can reduce the internal diameter of a drain pipe by 20–30%, turning a pipe that once drained quickly into a chronic slow-drain problem. No amount of plunging or snaking will fix this — the pipe itself needs descaling or replacement.

Signs you have a limescale buildup problem rather than a normal clog:

  • Multiple drains in the house are slow simultaneously
  • The drain was fully cleared by a plumber but slowed down again within 6 months
  • You can see white or yellow crusty deposits around drain openings
  • Your home was built before 1990 and has never had a drain cleaning service

The professional solution is hydro-jetting — a high-pressure water stream (up to 4,000 PSI) that scours the inside of the pipe clean. It's also the best maintenance service for Calgary homes with hard water, typically every 3–5 years.

When to Call a Plumber Instead

DIY methods handle the majority of household drain clogs. Call a licensed Calgary plumber when you see any of these:

  • Multiple slow drains at once — this means the clog is in the main sewer line, not the individual drain. Snaking at the sink won't help.
  • Gurgling sounds from other fixtures (toilets, sinks) when you run water elsewhere — air is being displaced by a blockage further down the system.
  • Sewage smell coming from drains — could indicate a broken pipe, a dry P-trap, or a sewer line problem.
  • Water backing up in the basement floor drain when you flush upstairs — sewer line blockage, needs immediate attention.
  • Two failed DIY attempts — if the plunger and snake didn't work, the clog is likely past the accessible portion of the pipe or is a root intrusion or collapsed section.
  • Under-slab issues — if you have a slab-on-grade home in south Calgary (common in communities like Shawnessy, Midnapore, Douglasdale), a slab leak is a possibility and requires camera inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Drano to unclog my drain?

Chemical drain cleaners work on some organic clogs (hair, soap) but not on mineral buildup, grease, or foreign objects. The bigger concern is damage: they can soften PVC pipe fittings, corrode older metal traps, and are dangerous if they splash back. If a chemical cleaner doesn't clear the clog on the first try, do not use a plunger afterward — the caustic liquid will splash on you. Flush with water first, then try mechanical methods.

How do I unclog a drain with baking soda and vinegar?

Pour ½ cup of baking soda into the drain, followed immediately by ½ cup of white vinegar. Cover the drain opening and wait 15 minutes — the CO₂ bubbles loosen light soap scum and grease. Flush with hot water. This method is best for maintenance on a partially-slow drain, not a fully blocked one.

Why is my drain slow even after I cleared the clog?

If a drain slows again within weeks of being cleared, it usually means: (a) you only partially cleared the clog and it reformed, (b) there's a secondary clog deeper in the line, or (c) Calgary's hard water has left mineral deposits that are now catching hair and debris faster than a clean pipe would. Option (c) is best addressed with professional hydro-jetting.

Is it safe to use a drain snake on PVC pipes?

Yes, with some care. PVC is relatively soft, so use a plastic-coated snake tip and avoid forcing the snake when you hit resistance — rotate and pull back rather than pushing hard. If in doubt, a professional drain auger is larger and harder to manoeuvre in the tight bends of residential PVC.
